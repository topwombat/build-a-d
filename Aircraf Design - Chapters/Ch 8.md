# Chapter 8 — *Crew Station, Passengers, and Payload*

### What this chapter is *really* doing

This chapter is not about comfort.

It is about **irreversibility**.

Once you place:

* eyes
* seats
* cargo
* weapons
* doors
* escape paths

…your aircraft’s geometry, CG envelope, and structural layout become **locked in**.

This is the chapter where:

> *the airplane stops being a machine and becomes a vehicle.*

---

## The hidden thesis

> **Human geometry and payload geometry dominate fuselage design more than aerodynamics ever will.**

If Chapters 6–7 tested whether your airplane could be drawn honestly, Chapter 8 tests whether it can be **used without redesigning it**.

## 9.1 Introduction — scope discipline

**Explicit**

* Conceptual design does *not* require detailed cockpit layout
* But basic geometry *must* be right early
* Otherwise payload integration will force a redesign

**Tacit context**

* This is a warning from experience
* Most late-stage redesigns come from payload and crew integration, not aerodynamics

The authors are telling you where programs bleed money.

## 9.2 Crew Station (the fuselage’s anchor point)

### Vision requirements (the real driver)

They emphasize:

* forward visibility during approach and landing
* over-nose vision
* over-side vision
* upward visibility (fighters, transports)

Figures 9.1 and 9.2 show:

* pilot eye reference point
* sight lines
* grazing angles
* clearance requirements

**Tacit context**

* Vision requirements *shape the nose*
* Nose shape drives:

  * drag
  * radar signature
  * cockpit placement
  * CG location

This is why “sleek noses” so often die on the runway.

---

### Anthropometrics (95th percentile pilot)

They explicitly size for:

* 5th–95th percentile pilots
* helmet clearance
* seat travel
* control reach

**Tacit context**

* You are not designing for *your* body
* You are designing for a distribution

Designing for extremes is a structural and geometric constraint, not a courtesy.

---

### Ejection seats & escape systems

They discuss:

* ejection seat envelopes
* speed and altitude limits
* canopy clearance
* crew capsules (e.g., FB-111, XB-70)

**Tacit context**

* Escape systems dominate cockpit height
* Cockpit height dominates fuselage cross-section
* Fuselage cross-section dominates drag and weight

Survivability is expensive but non-negotiable.

They emphasize:

* max seats per aisle
* aisle spacing
* door spacing

**Tacit context**

* Regulations are geometry constraints
* You don’t “optimize around” them

This is where FARs silently shape airplanes.

---

### Passenger mass assumptions

They assume:

* ~180 lb per passenger (dressed)
* ~40–60 lb baggage

**Tacit context**

* Payload assumptions drive:

  * CG range
  * floor loading
  * landing gear placement

Bad assumptions here poison stability later.

## 9.5 Weapons Carriage (CG tyranny)

This section is about **mass, moment, and release physics**.

### CG proximity

They stress:

* weapons must be near CG
* otherwise trim and stability are destroyed during release

**Tacit context**

* Payload *movement* matters as much as payload mass
* Dynamic CG shifts are more dangerous than static ones

---

### External vs internal carriage

Figures 9.5–9.8 show:

* rails
* ejectors
* pylons
* semi-submerged
* conformal
* internal bays
* rotary launchers

**Tacit context**

* External carriage is easy and deadly to performance
* Internal carriage is hard and geometrically expensive

Stealth, drag, and payload flexibility are in permanent tension.

---

### Release clearances

They emphasize:

* minimum angular clearances
* worst-case attitudes
* engine ingestion risks

**Tacit context**

* Release physics is violent and unforgiving
* Weapons integration failures are catastrophic, not annoying

---

## 9.6 Gun Installation (impulse physics)

They discuss:

* recoil forces
* yaw moments
* centerline placement
* gas ingestion
* muzzle flash

Figure 9.9 shows the M61 Vulcan installation.

**Tacit context**

* Guns are not “payload”
* They are dynamic structural loads

This is why fighters are designed *around* their guns, not with them added later.
