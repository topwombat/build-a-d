# Chapter 11 — *Aerodynamics*

### What this chapter is *really* doing

This chapter is **not** teaching fluid mechanics.

It is teaching you how to:

* estimate aerodynamic forces accurately enough to size the aircraft
* decompose drag into actionable components
* understand *which aerodynamic effects matter at conceptual design time*

The chapter’s job is **model selection**, not perfect prediction.

---

## The hidden thesis

> **Aerodynamics in conceptual design is about structured approximation, not precision.**

If you try to be exact here, you will be wrong *and* slow.

---

## 12.1 Introduction — what changes now

**Explicit**

* Previous chapters used rough aerodynamic estimates
* This chapter refines those estimates using physics-informed models
* Drag is the central focus

**Tacit context**

* From here on, geometry is assumed fixed

They explain:

* pressure drag (separation, bluffness)
* skin-friction drag (viscous shear)

**Tacit context**

* Pressure drag is usually geometry failure
* Skin friction is usually surface quality and wetted area

You fix these with different tools.

## 12.4 Lift (what creates it, what destroys it)

### Lift curve slope

Figures 12.4–12.6 show:

* effect of camber

* effect of Mach number
* sweep penalties

**Tacit context**

* Lift slope reductions are cumulative
* Sweep, compressibility, low AR all quietly tax lift

You don’t lose lift catastrophically — you lose it death-by-a-thousand-cuts.

* Prandtl-Glauert correction

**Tacit context**

* Transonic effects arrive early and unevenly

* (C_{L,\max}) vs aspect ratio

**Tacit context**

* High-speed aircraft pay for speed with poor low-speed lift

Stall speed constraints re-enter forcefully here.

## High-lift devices (geometry-based cheating)

Figures 12.16–12.18 show:

* flaps
* slats
* slots
* Fowler systems

Tables quantify:

* incremental ( \Delta C_{L,\max} )

**Tacit context**

* High-lift devices do not create free lift
* They trade:

  * complexity
  * weight
  * drag
  * maintenance
    for lower stall speed

This is how transport aircraft reconcile speed with runway limits.

## 12.6 Induced Drag (the lift penalty)

They derive:

* induced drag as a function of:

  * lift coefficient
  * aspect ratio
  * efficiency factor

**Tacit context**

* Induced drag dominates low-speed, high-lift regimes
* High AR wings buy efficiency but cost structure

This is where wing sizing tradeoffs become visible.

## Transonic design philosophy (quietly important)
