# Chapter 10 — *Landing Gear and Subsystems*

### What this chapter is *really* doing

This chapter is not about landing gear dimensions.

It is about **designing the parts of the aircraft that experience the worst loads, the most cycles, and the least sympathy**.

In flight, airplanes are elegant.
On the ground, airplanes are *mistreated machinery*.

---

## The hidden thesis

> **Most aircraft damage happens on the ground, and landing gear geometry determines whether it’s survivable or career-ending.**

This chapter teaches you how to:

* keep the airplane upright
* keep it controllable
* keep it operable
* keep it maintainable

---

## 11.1 Introduction — why landing gear dominates ground behavior

**Explicit**

* Landing gear must:

  * support the aircraft
  * absorb energy
  * provide stability
  * allow braking and steering
* Poor gear design leads to:

  * ground loops
  * nose-overs
  * structural damage
  * runway excursions

**Tacit context**

* Landing gear design is *risk mitigation*
* Elegant flight characteristics mean nothing if the aircraft is unusable on the ground

---

## 11.2 Landing Gear Arrangements (geometry = behavior)

### Tricycle vs taildragger

Figures show:

* tricycle gear
* conventional (tailwheel) gear
* quad gear

**Tacit context**

* Tricycle gear:

  * easier ground handling
  * better forward visibility
  * higher nose weight sensitivity
* Taildragger:

  * lighter
  * simpler
  * much less forgiving

This is a **pilot workload decision**, not just a structural one.

---

### Gear location relative to CG

They emphasize:

* main gear slightly aft of CG
* nose gear far enough forward to prevent nose-over
* lateral spacing for roll stability

**Tacit context**

* CG margin on the ground is as important as in flight
* Bad placement leads to:

  * porpoising
  * tipping
  * braking instability

This is geometry, not aerodynamics.

### Tip-over and nose-over

Figures illustrate:

* static tip-over angle
* braking-induced nose-over

**Tacit context**

* These are *pure geometry problems*
* No amount of braking finesse fixes bad geometry

The aircraft must tolerate **worst-case pilot behavior**.

---

### Track width and wheelbase

They discuss:

* minimum track width
* wheelbase sizing
* tradeoffs with weight and drag

**Tacit context**

* Wider gear:

  * better stability
  * heavier
  * harder to retract
* Narrow gear:

  * lighter
  * cheaper
  * dangerous in crosswinds

This is a stability–packaging trade, not a performance one.
